Section 2
Strike Selection — Keep the Instrument Consistent
➤
Selection Protocol

After the signal, first select the correct monthly expiry, then select a liquid CE or PE strike whose option premium is as close as practical to ₹800, generally within the ₹600–₹800 range.

Calendar DateMonthly Expiry to SelectPractical Rule
1st through 19thCurrent-month expiryUse a strike from the current month whose premium is generally within ₹600–₹800 and as close as practical to ₹800.
20th through month-endNext-month expiryRollover to the next month and select a liquid strike in the same preferred premium zone.
Monthly Expiry Rollover Rule: Till and including the 19th, trade current-month strikes. From and including the 20th, trade next-month strikes. The date rule applies to both CE and PE selections.

Preferred

A liquid strike in the target premium band with a reasonable bid–ask spread and executable quantity.

Avoid

Far OTM contracts, poor liquidity, unusually wide spreads, or delaying so long that the intended move is chased.

  • Check the calendar date before selecting every new strike—especially when switching around the 19th/20th boundary.
  • Use the same expiry and premium-selection discipline for both CE and PE.
  • Do not keep changing strikes because another premium appears more exciting.
  • If no suitable liquid strike exists, execution quality and risk come before forcing a trade.
Section 3
The 6% Trailing Stop-Loss — Your Protective Seat Belt
➤
Risk Protocol

A 6% trailing stop-loss is calculated from the option premium, not from the Bank Nifty or Nifty spot value.

Premium₹800
6% Distance₹48
Initial Reference₹752

Illustration only: If the premium is ₹800, 6% equals ₹48. A simple initial reference would be around ₹752. As the option premium rises, the stop should trail upward according to the execution platform’s mechanism; it should not be loosened downward.

  • Confirm whether your broker’s trailing order is supported and how it behaves.
  • Do not widen or cancel the stop because a loss feels uncomfortable.
  • A gap or fast premium move can produce an exit worse than the stop reference.
  • If the trailing SL exits you before a new signal, remain out of that position and wait for the next valid signal; do not re-enter from emotion.
Section 4
What to Do When the Opposite Signal Appears
➤
Reversal Protocol
  1. Recognise the new opposite signal.
  2. Close the existing position first. Do not wait for it to return to your entry price.
  3. Confirm the applicable monthly expiry: current month through the 19th, next month from the 20th onward.
  4. Select the opposite option near the target premium zone.
  5. Enter the new side and activate the 6% trailing SL.
  6. Record the switch, then return attention to the next signal.
Never hold CE and PE together to avoid accepting a loss. That changes a simple switch into an unmanaged hedge and breaks the 4.1 execution rule.
Section 5
Why the Framework Can Operate in Trending and Sideways Markets
➤
Outcome Structure

The framework does not require the trader to label the market first. It uses smaller controlled exits as the cost of remaining available for a larger directional move.

During Sideways Movement

Direction may switch repeatedly. The objective is to keep each failed attempt controlled; several small losses remain possible.

During a Strong Trend

The active position may expand while the trailing stop protects progress, allowing one larger move to offset several smaller attempts.

Designed outcome pattern: Small Loss + Small Profit + Big Profit. This is a framework objective, not a guaranteed daily sequence.

A controlled loss is the occasional “EMI” paid for participation—but only when it stays inside the predefined risk process. Frequent losses, slippage and costs can still accumulate.

➤

During execution, you do not need to add candlestick patterns, chart patterns, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Gann Theory, Elliott Waves, open interest, option-chain analysis, news predictions or another formula.

ThePearlMind is described as processing the market through 15 dimensions developed within the Black Pearl Vaults. The trader-facing layer is intentionally simpler: receive signal, execute, protect, repeat.

Simple does not mean easy. The analytical burden is reduced, but emotional discipline, correct order placement and risk control are still required.
Section 7
Discipline Rules — What Must Never Change Mid-Trade
➤
  • Do not ignore an opposite signal because the open trade is losing.
  • Do not book a winner only because a small profit looks attractive.
  • Do not increase quantity to recover the previous loss.
  • Do not widen the 6% trailing risk setting.
  • Do not mix extra indicators after entering.
  • Do not judge the method from one trade; audit a meaningful sample.
  • Do not trade with capital whose loss would disturb essential expenses.
  • Include brokerage, taxes, slippage and execution differences in every review.
